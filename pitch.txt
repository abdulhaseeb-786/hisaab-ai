HOW TO READ THIS
- SAY   = the words (about 130 words a minute; each beat is timed).
- DO    = what the demo driver does on the laptop/phone at that moment.
- SLIDE = what the slide behind you shows. One idea per slide; the slide supports, never competes.
- Two roles: SPEAKER (stands, talks to the jury) and DRIVER (runs the app, says only the demo lines).
  One person can do both; then hold the phone and speak to it yourself.
- [FILL] = a fact only your team can give. Do not invent it on stage.
- Demo numbers change every day (the demo shop runs up to today). Say what is ON SCREEN, not what is written here.

=====================================================================
JURY Q&A (2 minutes) — SHORT ANSWERS, 15–20 SECONDS EACH
=====================================================================
Rule: answer in one or two sentences, then stop. If a question is about something not built yet,
say "not yet" plainly and give the next step.

Q: "Is this real data?"
A: "No, it's a simulated shop with a full year of sales, and it's labelled 'Demo data' on every screen.
    It's built to load a real shop's sales and products from a CSV file, and that's our pilot."

Q: "How accurate is the forecast?"
A: "On the simulated shop, our calendar-aware model beats the simple averages, but simulated accuracy
    proves nothing about real shops, so we don't claim it. We're testing it on a public dataset of
    real retail sales next." [If you run the public benchmark before Dec 2, quote that number instead.]

Q: "What if the AI makes a mistake, or invents a number?"
A: "It can't write anything by itself. The shopkeeper confirms every entry. An amount the AI suggests is
    dropped unless it was actually said, and every price and balance comes from the books, not the AI."

Q: "What about noise in the shop, accents, Punjabi?"
A: "It understands mixed Urdu, Punjabi and English. When it's unsure, it shows choices instead of guessing,
    and he can always correct the card or type it."

Q: "What about privacy? You're recording customers' names."
A: "Voice goes to an online AI only after the owner says yes; it's off by default.
    No messages are sent from the app at all: no WhatsApp, no SMS. The data belongs to the shop."

Q: "No internet?"
A: "Everything except voice-to-text works offline: typed Urdu, the khata, the plan, the three jobs.
    Voice needs internet for now."

Q: "Why would a shopkeeper pay? Competitors?"
A: "Khata apps record what happened. We tell him what to do: what to buy, which price to fix,
    who to collect from. And speaking is faster than any app's typing. [FILL: model]."

Q: "What does one shop cost you to run?"
A: "A voice entry is one short AI call; most screens use no AI at all. [FILL: your estimate per shop
    per month, if asked. If you don't know: 'Low enough to be free at pilot scale; we'll measure it in the pilot.']"

Q: "How did you validate this with shopkeepers?"
A: [FILL] Only answer with real conversations. If you have none: "Not enough yet. That's exactly
    what the five-shop pilot is for."
